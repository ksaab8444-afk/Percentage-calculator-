Formulas & Methods

How to Calculate Percentages

Understand the mathematical principles behind percentage calculations with our clear, standardized formulas and explanations.

Concept

What is a Percentage?

The word percentage originates from the Latin per centum, meaning "by the hundred." A percentage is simply a ratio or fraction expressed with a denominator of 100, represented by the symbol %.

1% = 1 ÷ 100 = 0.01
50% = 50 ÷ 100 = 0.50
100% = 100 ÷ 100 = 1.00
Expressing any fraction as parts per 100
Mode 1

Percentage of a Number (X% of Y)

To calculate X% of Y, divide the percentage by 100 to convert it into a decimal fraction, and then multiply by the total number.

Result = (X ÷ 100) × Y
Example: 20% of 150 = (20 ÷ 100) × 150 = 0.2 × 150 = 30
Mode 2

X is What Percent of Y?

To find the proportion that X represents of a total Y, divide X by Y to get a decimal fraction, then multiply by 100.

Percentage = (X ÷ Y) × 100
Example: 30 of 150 = (30 ÷ 150) × 100 = 0.2 × 100 = 20%
Mode 3

Percentage Increase

Subtract the original starting value from the new value, divide the net difference by the original value, and multiply by 100.

Increase = ((New - Original) ÷ Original) × 100
Example: 100 to 150 = ((150 - 100) ÷ 100) × 100 = 50% increase
Mode 4

Percentage Decrease

Subtract the new value from the original value, divide the difference by the original value, and multiply by 100.

Decrease = ((Original - New) ÷ Original) × 100
Example: 150 to 100 = ((150 - 100) ÷ 150) × 100 = 33.33% decrease
Mode 5

Percentage Difference

Used when comparing two values without an established reference baseline. Divide the absolute difference by the average of the two numbers.

Difference = (|A - B| ÷ ((A + B) ÷ 2)) × 100
Example: 100 & 120 = (|100 - 120| ÷ 110) × 100 = 18.18%

Support & Answers

Frequently Asked Questions

Get direct answers to common questions regarding percentage formulas, decimal handling, and calculations.

How do I calculate a percentage?

To calculate what percentage one number (X) represents of another (Y), divide X by Y, then multiply the quotient by 100:

Percentage = (X ÷ Y) × 100

Conversely, to find what X% of a number Y is, convert the percentage into a decimal by dividing by 100, then multiply by Y: (X ÷ 100) × Y.

What is 20% of 100?

20% of 100 is 20.

Because percentages are parts per hundred, any percentage of 100 is equal to the percentage number itself. Mathematically: (20 ÷ 100) × 100 = 0.20 × 100 = 20.

How do I calculate percentage increase?

To calculate percentage increase between an original value and a new higher value:

  1. Subtract the original value from the new value to find the absolute gain: New - Original.
  2. Divide that gain by the original baseline value: Gain ÷ Original.
  3. Multiply by 100 to convert to a percentage: ((New - Original) ÷ Original) × 100.

For example, if rent increases from $1,000 to $1,150, the increase is ((1,150 - 1,000) ÷ 1,000) × 100 = 15%.

How do I calculate percentage decrease?

To calculate percentage decrease between an original starting value and a new lower value:

  1. Subtract the new value from the original value to find the reduction: Original - New.
  2. Divide that reduction by the original starting baseline: Reduction ÷ Original.
  3. Multiply by 100 to express as a percentage: ((Original - New) ÷ Original) × 100.

For example, if an item drops from $150 to $100, the percentage decrease is ((150 - 100) ÷ 150) × 100 = 33.33%.

What is the difference between percentage difference and percentage change?

Percentage Change (increase or decrease) is directional and requires a definitive starting baseline (an "original" vs. a "new" value). The denominator is always the starting value.

Percentage Difference is non-directional. It is used when comparing two concurrent values where neither is considered the baseline (such as product prices at two competing stores, or heights of two buildings). The denominator is the average of the two numbers:

Percentage Difference = (|A - B| ÷ ((A + B) ÷ 2)) × 100

Can I calculate percentages with decimals and negative numbers?

Yes! All 5 modes on this calculator support decimal numbers (such as 12.5% or $89.95) and negative inputs. Our calculation engine handles floating-point math cleanly, rounding accurately to up to 4 decimal places without computer binary rounding artifacts.
